the key Find a Grave pages, the 1919 newspaper and Marjorie's death certificate myself, marked
"(checked)" below. None of this is on Ancestry. It's applied to the website by `tools/research.py`,

Confidence: **High** = original record, newspaper or published genealogy that matches on several
points. **Med** = a user-submitted memorial or tree that fits everything we know. **Low** = a lead only.

## Open questions

1. **Is Thomas M. Harkins really William Andrew Harkins's son?** William's memorial, compiled by a descendant, lists nine children (Mary G. 1888, Helen 1889, William J. 1893, Esther 1894, Sylvester 1896, Grace 1897, Ruth 1898, Rose 1901, Daniel Joseph 1904). Thomas isn't among them.
   - The whole Wilmington and Tyrone line hangs on this link.
   - Thomas's 1943 Pennsylvania death certificate and the 1900 census both name his parents. Both are already hints on his Ancestry page, behind the U.S. records paywall. Ancestry's PA death certificates are free to Pennsylvania residents.
2. **Agnes Dougherty Henwood** isn't among the survivors in her father's Dec 1927 obituary, yet the 1930 census in the tree has her alive in Pennsauken.
3. **Joseph H. Perret's parents and Mary Martin's father:** the 1919 marriage license application names both sets of parents (FamilySearch, needs login). So would his WWI draft card.
4. **Marjorie Schuler's burial town:** the certificate's word after "Calvary Cemetery" is unclear. The Dymond genealogy says Olyphant.
